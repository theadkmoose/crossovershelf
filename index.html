      Scoring methodology

      Every book gets a 0–100 score built from its real award history, weighted by how far the honor reaches outside its home category. On the Award Crossovers shelf, the score is a straight average of a Literary-establishment reading and a Genre-fandom reading — that's the bridge bar on each card. The King and r/Fantasy shelves use a single consensus score, since they're ranking within one world rather than across two.

      • +30–35 pts — winning a top-tier mainstream prize (Booker, Pulitzer, National Book Award) or a top-tier genre prize (Hugo, Nebula)
      • +15–20 pts — shortlist / finalist / major secondary honor (Women's Prize, Arthur C. Clarke Award, World Fantasy Award)
      • +5–15 pts — canonical reputation: widely taught, frequently cited as genre-defining, or carried by an author's broader prize record
      • baseline — general critical and reader reception, aggregated from published reviews and reader-rating consensus
      S85–100 — canon-defining crossover
      A70–84 — major, widely recognized
      B55–69 — solid, notable standing
      C40–54 — modest crossover appeal
      D25–39 — niche or divisive
      Fbelow 25 — little crossover recognition

      This is an editorial ranking system built for browsing, not a peer-reviewed metric — treat ties and near-ties as a wash.
